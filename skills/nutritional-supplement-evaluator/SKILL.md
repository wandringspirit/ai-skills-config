# Nutritional Supplement Evaluator & Ingredient Audit Framework (`skills/nutritional-supplement-evaluator`)

An authoritative, evidence-based methodology for evaluating dietary supplements, protein powders, meal replacements, and wellness formulas. Combines principles from clinical nutrition (Examine.com, ISSN, WHO/FAO) with consumer protection economics to determine whether a product is legitimately worth purchasing.

## 2. Pillar 1: Ingredient Purity & Macronutrient Density

### 2.1 The Protein Purity Formula
Never judge a powder by scoop size or total tub weight. Calculate actual nutrient density:

$$\text{Protein Purity \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Grams of Protein per Serving}}{\text{Total Serving Weight (g)}} \right) \times 100$$

- **High-Grade Pure Whey Isolate / Concentrate:** 75% – 90% purity.
- **Acceptable Plant Blends / Standard Powders:** 60% – 75% purity.
- **Low-Grade Fillers / Gimmick Shakes:** **< 50% purity** (means > 50% of what the consumer pays for is cheap carbs, maltodextrin, vegetable fats, and gums).

### 2.2 Filler & Bulking Agent Audit
Scan the label for low-cost bulking agents used to create mouthfeel and weight at minimal manufacturing cost:
- **Maltodextrin / Corn Syrup Solids / Starch:** Cheap, ultra-high glycemic index (GI 85–105) carbs that cause insulin spikes and dilute active ingredients.
- **Palm Fat Powder / Hydrogenated Oils:** Inexpensive fats added to mimic dairy creaminess without genuine nutritional quality.
- **Texturizers & Gums (Guar, Xanthan, Carrageenan):** Harmless in trace amounts, but heavily used in low-protein mixes to fake milkshake thickness.
- **Amino Spiking / Nitrogen Fraud:** Check if non-essential amino acids (glycine, taurine, glutamine) or creatine are added to falsely elevate Kjeldahl nitrogen test results.

## 3. Pillar 2: The Cost-Per-Gram True Value Formula

Products often look inexpensive upfront (e.g., ₹599 or $15 for a tub), but deliver negligible active compounds.

### Calculation Steps:
1. **Total Net Protein in Container:**
   $$\text{Total Net Protein (g)} = \text{Servings per Container} \times \text{Protein per Serving (g)}$$
2. **True Cost per Gram:**
   $$\text{Cost per Gram of Protein} = \frac{\text{Retail Purchase Price}}{\text{Total Net Protein (g)}}$$

### Benchmark Comparison (India Market Reference):
- **Whole Cow Milk (Toned):** ~₹1.60 – ₹1.80 per gram of protein (plus natural B12, bioavailable calcium).
- **Skimmed Milk Powder (Amul/Sagar SMP):** ~₹1.10 – ₹1.30 per gram of protein (35% natural protein).
- **Certified Unflavored Whey Concentrate 80% (Nakpro, As-It-Is, Nutrabay):** ~₹1.80 – ₹2.20 per gram of pure, high-leucine protein (80% purity).
- **Eggs:** ~₹1.00 – ₹1.20 per gram of complete, high-BV protein.
- **Overpriced Gimmick / MLM Powders:** Often exceed **₹3.00 – ₹6.00+ per gram of low-grade (~40% purity) protein**.

## 4. Pillar 3: Formulation Quality & Bioavailability

- **Protein Source:**
  - *Tier 1 (Optimal MPS):* Whey protein isolate/concentrate, micellar casein (high leucine, complete EAA profile).
  - *Tier 2 (Good Plant Blends):* Pea + Rice isolate, soy protein isolate (good PDCAAS, lower leucine trigger).
  - *Tier 3 (Cheap Commodity Base):* Skimmed milk powder rebottled at 3x markup.
- **Micronutrient Bioavailability:**
  - *Vitamin D:* Cholecalciferol (D3) is vastly superior in raising serum 25(OH)D levels compared to cheap Ergocalciferol (D2).
  - *Calcium:* Calcium carbonate requires stomach acid and has lower elemental absorption compared to milk calcium or calcium citrate malate.

## 5. Pillar 4: Sourcing, Distribution & Gray-Market Authenticity

When analyzing e-commerce sellers offering discounted supplements:
1. **MLM / Direct-Selling Gray Markets:**
   - Multi-Level Marketing brands (Herbalife, Amway, Modere) strictly prohibit retail e-commerce sales.
   - Products listed on third-party sites (independent Shopify/WooCommerce stores) are gray-market inventory dumped by distributors desperate to hit volume quotas, near-expiry stock, or counterfeits.
   - Manufacturer warranties, tamper guarantees, and batch traceability are forfeited.
2. **Third-Party Testing & Contamination Audits:**
   - Look for certifications: Informed-Choice, NSF Certified for Sport, Labdoor, Clean Label Project.
   - Cross-reference with clinical literature for hepatotoxicity, heavy metal contamination (lead, arsenic), and adulteration.

## 6. Step-by-Step Evaluation Protocol

When a user submits a product link or asks "is this worth it?":
1. **Extract Core Data**: Container weight, serving size, servings count, price, protein per serving, total calories, complete ingredient list.
2. **Calculate Metrics**:
   - Purity percentage.
   - Total net active nutrient in container.
   - Cost per gram of active compound.
3. **Audit Ingredients**: Identify active sources vs. fillers (maltodextrin, palm fat, artificial flavoring, thickeners).
4. **Audit Retailer & Authenticity**: Verify whether the seller is an authorized distributor or gray-market broker.
5. **Formulate the Verdict**:
   - Direct Bottom Line (Worth it vs. Not worth it).
   - What the product actually is in plain terms (demystify marketing jargon).
   - The economic rip-off vs. value calculation.
   - Superior, cheaper whole-food or certified supplement alternatives.
